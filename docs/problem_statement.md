# Problem Statement

## Target users

Wheelchair users and other mobility-constrained students, staff and visitors who need to
travel between campus buildings using only step-free, ramp- or elevator-accessible paths.

## Evidence

Campus accessibility audits at many institutions (e.g. published disability-access reviews
by university estates/facilities offices) consistently find that a meaningful share of
direct walking routes between buildings include stairs, kerbs or unramped slopes, forcing
wheelchair users onto long, undocumented detours that able-bodied routing (e.g. generic
map apps) does not account for. Students who require accessible routes frequently report
being late to class or having to ask staff for directions because no accessible route map
is published (illustrative synthesis for this teaching example; a real submission must
cite specific, current, credible sources per APA 7).

## Urgency and social value

Inaccessible or undocumented routing directly affects a student's ability to attend classes
on time, access facilities independently, and participate equally in campus life. An agent
that reliably finds the shortest *accessible* route, not just the shortest route, removes a
daily barrier and supports independent navigation without relying on ad hoc help from others.

## SDG alignment

- **SDG 4 (Quality Education)** — supports equal access to timely, independent participation
  in classes and campus facilities.
- **SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities)** — directly addresses a mobility-access inequality between
  wheelchair users and other campus users.
- **SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities)** — contributes to accessible, inclusive
  physical infrastructure and wayfinding.
